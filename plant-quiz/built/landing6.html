Plant care guide

Plants can be easier than they look. These six ideas fit most houseplants, big or small.

Check the soil before you water.

Push a finger about 2 inches into the soil. If it feels dry, water. If it’s still damp, wait a few days.

Bright, indirect light is a good start.

Many houseplants like bright light that doesn’t hit them directly. Strong sun through glass can burn leaves. Watch where light falls through the day.

Long vines can be normal.

Climbing plants like Monstera grow long on purpose. In nature they climb trees. A pole or trellis can help hold them up.

Want help with your own plant?

Wait before you repot or trim.

A move is stressful for a plant. Give it a few weeks to settle before big changes. Repot later only if roots look crowded.

Waiting a little is often the kindest thing you can do.

Drainage holes help roots breathe.

Water needs a way out of the pot. Roots that sit wet can rot. No holes? Keep the plant in a plain pot with holes, set inside the pretty one.

Check if your plant is pet safe.

Many common houseplants can upset pets if chewed, and Monstera is one. Look up your plant’s name on the ASPCA plant list. If you’re unsure, keep it out of reach.

You don’t need to fix everything today. Start with one small check, like the soil. Your plant will show you more over time. If you’d like help with your own plant, answer a few questions.
